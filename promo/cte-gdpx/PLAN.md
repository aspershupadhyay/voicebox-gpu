# Catch the Error: GDPx, the AI exam where the best model scores 12%

**What it's for:** a Reel / Short (1080×1920, 68 s of voice + the ident, 75 s) on SemiAnalysis's GDPx benchmark
(released Nov 2, 2026; the post: x.com/SemiAnalysis_/status/2117075900575477855).
**Who it's for:** people who follow AI news, see "12%" and don't know what the test is.
**One question:** if the best AI scores 12%, what kind of test is this?

**Format:** "the number" (formats.md #5): one figure, taken apart, then the catch.
**World:** *the exam paper*, in the Cyclon house look (the owner asked for "exactly like Cyclon"): paper, ink and the
red pen, which here does what a red pen does on an exam: marks, grades and circles. Kraft-paper job folder for the
"real job". Ink scene only for the catch.
**Anchor image:** the line-up (the owner's v2 note): the labs' real marks in their real colours as app-icon tiles in a
ring, popping in one per 16th note, a score badge on each (OPEN on the open models), and **12%** in the middle, ringed
by the red pen on "twelve". It is the cover and comes back, small, for the payoff.
**The unique thing:** the real counts. 206 cells in 11 columns, one per field, at the real numbers from
gdpx.semianalysis.com/domains (RSI 10, chips 34, cyber 18, bio 13, finance 24, legal 20, quant 10, hardware 21,
materials 23, alignment 18, software 15). The word montage lights their columns.

## Platform psychology (what each line is doing)
- **0-2 s (all platforms):** text, motion and voice on frame 1: the sheet is sliding in under "The best AI in the
  world just took a new exam." The pen grades it on "twelve", the one sub hit. A superlative + a shockingly low
  number = a curiosity gap (how can the best be 12%?).
- **Reels (sends):** the share moment is the real leaderboard with "a third of the price" circled, and the ask is a
  one-word comment (which field falls first), which fuels the comment ranking.
- **Shorts (retention):** a cut every 1-3 s on the 120 BPM grid, the mode changes every ~10 s (paper → kraft →
  grid → ink catch → paper), and the payoff calls back to the opening sheet.
- **Trust:** the catch is sourced (zeros in finance and law, safety filters, no third-party rerun), so the hype
  doesn't outrun the facts.

## Facts and sources (checked 2026-11-04)
- gdpx.semianalysis.com (leaderboard): Opus 5.5 12.0% ($51.41/task), Gemini 4 Carbon 10.9% ($16.29), GPT-6 Astra
  9.6% ($53.99). "The GDPx score averages each model across all 11 domains… default score… average across 3 attempts."
  (The post's screenshot shows GPT 6.1 Sol at 8.3% / $9.78; the live site now says 8.4% / $9.80. We don't say it.)
- gdpx.semianalysis.com/domains: 11 domains, 206 tasks, 14 partners, the per-domain counts above.
- SemiAnalysis's write-up (newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/gdpx-measuring-frontier-model-capabilities): "The default
  time limit for a GDPx task is 8 hours, though some tasks had shorter time limits"; the finance task (Living Context)
  with a fund buying ~62% of a snack company for $880M plus an earnout, a ~4,000-file deal room; "Our finance and
  legal tasks are so challenging that basically every model scores exactly 0"; "certain models like Opus 5.5 and
  GPT-6 Astra have sandbagged overall scores due to their safety classifiers" (cyber, biology, alignment); chip
  designs scored against an expert reference; quant strategies against a reference strategy's P&L.
- "Unreleased" Gemini 4 Carbon: Dealroom's summary of the launch. "A third of the price": $16.29 vs $51.41.
- No third-party reproduction: AICoder's report ("not yet reproduced by a third party"), nothing newer found.
- Labelled as illustrations on screen: the quiz, the chip/P&L panel, the three tries = 33%.
