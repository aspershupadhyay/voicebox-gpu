## Caption
This satellite decides which photos of Earth we never see. 🛰️

At any moment about two thirds of Earth is under cloud, so most photos from space are just white, and a satellite
only gets a few minutes per pass to send its photos home.

1️⃣ 2020: ESA's Φ-sat-1 put a tiny AI chip on board to check every photo for cloud before sending (the chip took
36 hours of radiation at CERN first). Skipping the cloudy shots saves about 30% of the data.
2️⃣ Now Φ-sat-2, a shoebox-sized satellite (6U, launched Aug 2024), runs AI apps in orbit: ships, street maps, wildfires.
3️⃣ Fire satellites can send the alert first, before the full picture.

So, what's the catch? ESA's fire app sends nothing down when it finds no fire. If it ever mistakes smoke for cloud,
nobody on the ground sees it. (The smoke in the video is real: Punjab, 1 Nov 2026.)

Would you let it decide? Yes or no 👇
